**What problem does it solve, and for whom?**
It helps parents with limited time and resources prepare their children, aged 3–5, for school.
**What did you build?**
Many parents have limited time and knowledge to prepare their children for school. Our website makes it easy for them to help their child learn and develop, using exercises that are either pre-built by us or generated by the AI we've implemented. The AI creates tailored, creative exercises based on basic household items people already have at home.

**How do I run it?**
1. On the home screen, press "Start learning."
2. Choose whether to continue as a guest or log in (logging in allows your progress to be saved).
  -If you choose guest, you'll be asked for your child's name, then you can continue. Guest progress is not saved.
  -If you choose to log in, enter your email and choose a password. You can then add a child to your account in the account center with their name and age.
3. Next, choose whether to use the pre-built exercises or the AI-generated ones.
  -Pre-built exercises: choose between Letters, Numbers, or Motor Skills.
  -AI-generated exercises: tell it what household items you have, and it will create exercises based on that.
4. Track your child's progress in the "Progress" menu at the top.
5. View all activities in the "Activities" menu at the top.

**Ethical reflection - what are the risks of your tool? Who could it harm?**
The app collects minimal personal data — only a child's name and age — so a data breach would not expose any sensitive personal information. However, there is a risk on the content side: if a parent tells the AI they have a potentially dangerous item at home, such as a knife, the AI adds a warning saying it should be dull and used only under adult supervision, but it cannot actually enforce that supervision happens. If a parent doesn't follow the warning, a child could be harmed. This is a limitation of the AI's safety layer — it can advise, but it cannot guarantee real-world safety, especially since the app has no way of verifying who is actually present during an activity. A next step to reduce this risk would be to have the AI refuse to generate activities involving genuinely hazardous items altogether, rather than allowing them with a caveat.
